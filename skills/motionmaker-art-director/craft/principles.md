# Art direction principles (read on demand)

An art director owns **what it looks like and why**, and is the person who says no. On a film or an ad, they are accountable for one idea expressed consistently, not for how many effects fit in. Everything below serves that.

## 1. The process (agency and film practice), and our gates
| Stage | What the AD produces | Gate (the user approves before moving on) |
|---|---|---|
| Creative brief | objective, audience, single-minded message, tone, mandatories | "Is this what we are saying, to whom?" |
| Concept | one-sentence big idea + visual metaphor; 3 directions ("territories") | pick A / B / C |
| Style frames | key stills in the chosen look, on the real template | "Is this the world?" |
| Board / shot list | shot by shot: what we see, focal point, composition, motion, text, sound, emotion | "Is this the story?" |
| Motion test | the hero move or opening, rendered | "Does it move the way it should?" |
| Craft review | critique rounds against the checklist, with measurements | AD signs the poster frame |
| Delivery | the approved cut + the brief it came from | |

Never animate before the concept and a style frame are agreed: fixing a wrong idea in motion is the most expensive mistake.

## 2. The creative brief (ask like an AD, one decision per question)
- **Objective:** after watching, the viewer should *think / feel / do* what? (one verb)
- **Audience & context:** who, and where do they see it (muted autoplay in a feed, a conference screen, a product page)? This decides sound, text size, pace.
- **Single-minded message:** one sentence, ideally 8 words or fewer. If it needs "and", it is two videos.
- **Reason to believe:** the one proof (a number, the product doing the thing).
- **Tone:** three words, plus one "not" ("confident, warm, precise, *not* loud").
- **Mandatories and no-gos:** logo rules, brand colours and fonts, legal copy, things to avoid.
- **References and anti-references:** what they love and what they hate. Ask what *specifically* they like about a reference (the colour, the pace, the type?) and take the principle, never the footage. Do not copy another artist's work or use real trademarks/likenesses without permission.

## 3. The big idea
Test: can it be said in one sentence, and does a visual metaphor carry it? ("Export is a sealed, finished object." "Speed: everything snaps to the beat.") If you removed the logo, would it still feel like this brand? Prefer one strong idea executed with restraint over three half-ideas.

## 4. Visual language
**Hierarchy.** One hero per frame, then 2 to 3 levels. Test: grayscale + squint (see the checklist). Size, contrast, position and isolation create hierarchy; colour alone is the weakest.
**Composition.** Pick a structure on purpose: centred (formal, calm), thirds (dynamic), asymmetry with deliberate negative space (premium). Negative space is part of the design: under ~40% of the frame should be content. Keep essentials inside a 5% margin on every side (9:16: keep ~12% top and bottom clear of platform UI). Leading lines and eye-lines point *at* the focal point, not off the frame.
**Colour.** 60-30-10: dominant background, secondary/text, one accent for the single thing to notice. Text on background >= 4.5:1; accent >= 3:1 for large shapes. Colour carries emotion (warm = human/energetic, cool = calm/technical, black+gold = luxury) but only if the palette is restrained. A colour script plans how colour shifts across shots (cool setup, warm payoff).
**Type.** Max two families (one is usually enough); contrast comes from size and weight, not from more fonts. Pick a scale ratio (1.25 calm, 1.5 punchy). Display text: tight tracking (-2 to -4%); caps labels: open (+5 to +8%). About 40 characters per line on screen. Check the *longest* copy and the real installed font.
**Shape and texture.** Rounded = friendly, sharp = precise, grain/paper = human, glass/gradient = tech. Choose one family of shapes and repeat it.
**Light and camera (3D).** Motivated light: a warm key, a cool softer fill, a rim to separate from the background; keep a clear key-to-fill ratio for shape. Lens: 30 to 50 degrees field of view for product; wide exaggerates space, long compresses. The camera never parks; every move has a reason (reveal, follow, emphasise).

## 5. Motion language: personality becomes numbers
Every motion choice should answer "what does this direct the eye to, or what does it make the viewer feel?". If it answers neither, cut it.
| Personality | Enter | Stagger | Spring ratio | Holds | Cuts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calm / editorial | 20-28 f @24 | 3-4 f | 1.0 (no bounce) | long (x1.3) | slow dissolves, pushes |
| Premium / luxe | 24-30 f | 4 f | 1.0 | longest (x1.5) | dissolves, almost nothing moves at once |
| Confident / launch | 12-14 f | 2 f | 0.8 | normal | hard cuts on the beat, one orchestrated moment per shot |
| Playful | 10-12 f | 1 f | 0.5 | short | quick cuts, overshoot on shapes (never on text) |
| Mechanical / dev | 6-8 f | 1 f | 1.0 | short | hard cuts, stepped reveals |
| Cinematic | 24+ f | 3 f | 1.0 | long | slow push-in, cut on a sound hit |
One family of easing per piece. Secondary motion starts 100-200 ms after the hero and travels 50-70% as far. Ambient motion stays under 5% of the hero's travel. Contrast of fast and slow is rhythm; constant speed is monotony. Holds are design: let the payoff sit long enough to read (0.3 s per word + 0.5 s).

## 6. Story structure for short ads
Hook (0-3 s: the question or the striking image), Build (the problem or the setup), Payoff (the product moment, the thing the whole piece exists for), Close (logo + one next action). 6 s = hook+payoff; 15 s = adds build; 30 s adds a second beat. The first frame must work as a thumbnail; the last frame is the poster and the lingering memory; loops flow end to start.
Every shot gets one job. Cut anything that is not the hook, the build, the payoff or the close.

## 7. Platform conventions
Feed video autoplays muted: the message must work in silence (on-screen text, clear action). 1:1 is the safest in a feed; 9:16 needs top/bottom clear zones; 16:9 for sites and decks. Never flash more than 3 times a second. Keep brand clear space around the logo, never stretch or recolour it.

## 8. Giving and taking notes like an AD
Specific, about the work, tied to a frame ("at frame 84 the label competes with the cursor"), in the language of hierarchy, contrast, rhythm, restraint. Ask "what is the one thing to see here?" Kill your darlings: if a beautiful detail does not serve the idea, remove it.
