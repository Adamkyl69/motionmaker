# Directing illustration and borrowed animation

Borrowed art is the fastest way for a piece to look like a template. The art director's job is to make it look like it was drawn for this idea.

## Choosing
- **One family per piece.** Do not mix hand-drawn Open Peeps with flat Humaaans, or a 3D-ish Rive truck with line art. Pick the family that matches the look: line and ink for *editorial-calm*, *warm-craft*, *swiss-minimal*; flat colour for *playful-pop*, *bold-launch*; a single hero animation for *night-cinema*, *tech-mono*.
- **One hero asset, a supporting cast of at most two.** Everything else is type and space.
- **Fit the message, not the category.** A person is right when the idea is about people; a decorative blob is not a visual metaphor. Ask what the asset *says* in 0.2 seconds, silent and small.
- **Diversity on purpose.** When casting several characters, vary age, skin tone, hair and clothing deliberately; do not default to one look. Never use a character to stand in for a real person or a real customer.
- **Quality bar for a downloaded animation:** clean loop, readable at phone size, no dead frames, the motion has a point (anticipation, follow-through). Inspect it first; reject it if it is only decoration.

## Making it belong
- Recolour **clothes, props, backgrounds** to the palette: ink to the look's text colour, paper to its background, one accent for the hero object. Keep skin tones as drawn.
- Match line weight: if the type is heavy, thin hairline art looks lost; scale or choose another asset.
- Give it the stage's light: same background, a soft ground shadow (the template adds one), no drop shadows that fight the look.
- Do not stretch or crop art to fill a layout. Give it a box (the template uses 70% width and about two thirds of the height) and breathing room.

## Directing the motion
- Borrowed animation sets the tempo. Match the look's motion personality to it, or choose another asset; do not retime it wildly.
- Loops: the animation length (or a whole multiple) must equal the piece length, or the loop point jumps.
- Entrance and exit still follow the system: one arrival, one hold, one exit; the caption arrives after the art, not with it.
- Idle motion on static art stays small: breath about 1.4%, head bob under 1% of body height, sway about 1 degree. If it looks animated at first glance, it is too much.
- Sound hits go on the picture event, as always.

## Critique checks (add to the usual round)
1. Is it one family, one style? 2. Does the asset read in the grayscale and phone-thumbnail looktests? 3. Is the accent on the hero object only? 4. Does any asset edge touch the margin or collide with the caption? 5. Is anything illegible because art colours drifted into the text colour? 6. Does the asset make the message clearer or only prettier? If only prettier, cut it.

## Licence discipline
- Every asset is in the ledger before export; `ledger.mjs check` must PASS.
- CC BY (Rive community) needs a visible credit: put it in the end card or the post text, not only in a file.
- Do not compile downloaded Lottie files into a pack or a competing library; do not feed unDraw art to AI training; keep brand logos as the brand's.
- If the user says "I have a licence for a paid kit", record the plan and the terms URL, and ask whether video and ad use are covered.
